Which customers need you this week?

A customer health score built from first principles, for a fictional book of 80 accounts on an AI-enabled SaaS platform. It turns signals from the CRM and CS platform into a score, a priority list and a next action for every account.

Skill shown
Designing a health score, prioritising churn risk by revenue, and turning data into actions
The problem
80 accounts, £1.54m ARR and one CSM. Without a consistent score, attention goes to whoever shouts loudest, not to the revenue most at risk.
What I did
Chose signals that genuinely predict renewal, grouped and weighted them, set bands and hard caps, and linked each band and segment to a play
Result
£371k ARR (24%) flagged red or amber, a ranked top 10 to act on first, and 29 expansion candidates worth £576k ARR
Tools used
Excel (weighted scoring, ranking formulas), Claude (analysis and build), HTML/CSS, GitHub Pages

Where this fits. This is the simpler health score I built first, for a standard SaaS product. The AI agent health score builds on it for products where usage alone can’t tell you whether the AI is getting it right. I later moved this model out of Excel into SQL with weekly Slack alerts.

All accounts, names and figures are fictional. The signals and bands are based on how I've run health scoring in practice. The weightings are my proposed starting point.

Starting from first principles

A health score should answer one question: how likely is this customer to renew and grow? So I only kept signals that explain renewal, and grouped them by the reason a customer stays or leaves.

DimensionWeightSignalsWhy it matters
Adoption35%Workflows live vs contracted, last admin login, AI features in useCustomers renew what they use. AI adoption shows they're getting the newest value.
Relationship35%Customer Pulse score, days since they engaged, days since we contacted them, contacts mapped (sponsor, champion, 2+ admins)Sentiment and coverage. One contact is a single point of failure.
Support15%Open tickets, escalated ticketsUnresolved friction erodes trust, and escalations count most.
Commercial15%Overdue payment flagOften the earliest sign of budget pressure.

Each signal is scored 0 to 10 against clear thresholds, then combined into a score out of 10.

Bands, caps and plays

Red 1–4 Amber 5–6 Light green 7–8 Dark green 9–10

  • Hard caps: an overdue payment or two or more escalations caps the score at 6 (amber), however good everything else looks. A happy customer with an unpaid invoice isn't healthy.
  • Plays depend on band and segment. Every red or amber account gets an account review first. Then it's an executive call for enterprise accounts, a check-in for mid-market, and a check-in plus targeted self-help for SMB. Dark green accounts move to expansion and advocacy.
  • Priority ranks red and amber accounts by ARR × (10 − score) ÷ 10, weighted up when renewal is within 90 or 180 days. The first call goes to where the most revenue is at risk soonest.

What I'd do next with real data

The weights are a starting hypothesis, not the truth. With a real book, I'd score last year's accounts and check the scores against who actually churned or expanded. I'd then adjust the weights until red reliably means risk and dark green reliably means growth. I'd also review the thresholds each quarter with the team, so the score stays something CSMs trust and act on.
