# People discovery notes: Brightwater Clinics (FICTIONAL SAMPLE)

Eight voice interviews, 5 to 10 minutes each, held 7 to 18 September 2026, before configuration started. Each was recorded with the person's consent and transcribed by the AI note-taker the group has approved. Recordings were deleted after 30 days in line with the group's retention policy.

Interviewees are referred to by role and code. The group's managers see themes, not names, except where the person agreed (P4, the practice manager, agreed to be named as champion).

## P1: receptionist, worried about her job

- **How work flows today:** answers about 80 calls a day, books and moves appointments, checks patients in. Calls ring out while she's at the desk with someone.
- **Leak points:** the 8am to 10am peak; lunchtime when she covers alone. Voicemails "pile up by Monday".
- **Happy to hand over:** "moving appointments around all day"; chasing people who don't confirm.
- **Worries:** asked twice whether the AI agent would mean fewer reception hours. Said her hours were cut once before "when they brought in online booking". Also worried patients will think the clinic "doesn't care any more".
- **Go-to people:** Ruth (P4) for anything unusual.
- **Signals:** worried about her job; not sceptical about the technology; knows the regulars well.

## P2: receptionist, Quayside

- **How work flows today:** similar to P1. Uses web chat already and likes it.
- **Happy to hand over:** evening web chat and "the same five questions about parking and prices".
- **Worries:** that it will book new patients with the wrong clinician or the wrong length of appointment.
- **Signals:** enthusiastic; volunteered to test.

## P3: dental nurse and receptionist, Eastgate

- **Leak points:** dental emergencies on Monday mornings; patients with swelling or pain "who just leave a voicemail".
- **Worries:** that the AI agent won't know which dental pain is urgent.
- **Signals:** neutral; wants to see the urgent rules.

## P4: practice manager, the go-to person (agreed to be named)

- **How work flows today:** runs Quayside and is informally the person every clinic calls for "how do we do this". Holds the rules for new patient appointment types in her head and in a personal spreadsheet.
- **Leak points:** new patients booked into the wrong appointment type, then rebooked; recall lists only worked when someone has a quiet afternoon.
- **Happy to hand over:** recalls; out-of-hours messages; "explaining our cancellation policy forty times a week".
- **Worries:** "if it gets the booking rules wrong, I'll spend all my time fixing it." Wants to check the knowledge base herself.
- **Go-to people:** every receptionist interviewed named her.
- **Signals:** enthusiastic, influential and the go-to person. Single point of knowledge for booking rules.

## P5: physiotherapist, clinical lead, sceptical about the AI

- **How work flows today:** sees patients all day; returns calls at lunch about pain after treatment.
- **Worries:** "the minute it tells a patient their pain is normal, someone gets hurt." Has read about AI chat tools giving medical advice. Doubts it can tell a routine question from a clinical one.
- **Go-to people:** other physiotherapists across the group ask her clinical questions; she has a lot of influence with clinical staff.
- **Signals:** influential sceptic. Not against the admin side; strongly against anything near clinical triage.

## P6: dentist, Eastgate

- **Worries:** patient health details being visible to staff at other clinics.
- **Signals:** neutral; data protection is the concern.

## P7: receptionist, Hollins Cross

- **Leak points:** the clinic shares one phone line with the hygienist's room; calls are missed whenever the receptionist is chairside.
- **Signals:** keen; "anything that answers the phone is good".

## P8: recalls administrator, group office

- **How work flows today:** works recall lists for all 12 clinics; about a third get done in a normal month.
- **Happy to hand over:** sending reminders and booking straightforward recalls.
- **Worries:** what her role becomes if the AI agent does recalls.
- **Signals:** worried about her role; a natural reviewer for the recall rules.

## Themes

1. **Fear of replacement** at reception and in recalls (P1, P8). Linked to a past cut in hours.
2. **Clinical safety** is the line clinical staff care about (P5, P3). P5 is influential across the group.
3. **Booking rules sit with one person** (P4).
4. **Data access between clinics** (P6).
5. **Real appetite** for help with peaks, evenings and repeat questions (P2, P4, P7).

These feed the people risk register and champion map in the go-live pack.
