Ten takeaways from TSW 2026, and the three I keep coming back to

23 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short

Ten themes from TSW 2026, and three that stayed with me: proof of outcome moving before the sale, value engineering moving into services, and customer success learning to speak the language of value. The last one is the skill I most want to build.

  • Events
  • Value Realisation
  • Customer Success

Source

TSIA. TSW 2026 Recap: 10 AI Customer Experience Takeaways, 21 October 2026.

Why I picked this up

A good recap is the closest thing to attending a conference without the jet lag. I read TSIA’s write-up of Technology & Services World 2026 to see which themes ran through the whole event, rather than any single talk.

What it says, in brief

TSIA’s TSW 2026 Recap: 10 AI Customer Experience Takeaways summarises the event, held in Las Vegas in October 2026, with more than 80 sessions on the AI customer lifecycle. The message running through the keynotes was that customers now judge AI on proof of outcome, and the job of proving it has moved from sales into services.

The ten themes range from proof before the sale and hybrid outcome pricing to forward-deployed engineering and value literacy in customer success. Each comes with the speaker or session behind it and a link to the replay.

What stayed with me

Proof of outcome before the sale. J.B. Wood’s opening point, as the recap describes it, is that customers now expect to see results before they buy, and AI-native competitors can show them within weeks. That pulls services into the deal. It moves the hard work earlier than most organisations are set up for.

Value engineering moving into services. Thomas Lah’s argument is that the industry has become reasonably good at selling value but remains weak at proving it, and that proving it now belongs to services. A value model built during the sale becomes a commitment someone has to measure and report on after go-live. That someone is increasingly customer success.

Customer success needing to speak the language of value. This one feels the most personal. Value literacy means translating activity into the outcomes a client’s leaders care about. A CSM who reports meetings held and tickets closed is describing effort. A CSM who can say what that effort protected or saved is describing value. It’s the skill I most want to keep building, and it’s the thinking behind my compliance renewal value model.

A fourth theme sits under all three. Writing about scaling AI in support, the recap says "the numbers follow the operating model". AI at scale is an operating model question, not a tooling one. The tools matter far less than the data and the plays behind them.

In professional services

The recap describes renewal becoming a checkpoint inside continuous value realisation, rather than a single event at the end of a contract. In a compliance professional services business serving schools and trusts, that’s already true, whether or not anyone calls it that. With long contracts and break clauses, the real checkpoints are break dates and budget dates. A school setting its budget is making a renewal decision, whatever the contract says.

So the value conversation belongs before those dates, not at the end of the term. That means knowing each client’s budget cycle as well as their contract dates, and arriving with evidence of what the service has done for them.

Value literacy needs a services translation, too. In compliance the client owns the outcome, so value is risk reduced and assurance given, not a guarantee. The language has to be precise, because overclaiming in this sector costs trust that’s hard to win back.

In a tech role

Two trends from the recap are ones I want to understand better. The first is hybrid outcome pricing: a guaranteed base plus a share tied to results. It changes what customer success is accountable for, because the results start to carry revenue directly.

The second is forward-deployed engineering becoming a standard role. If engineers embed with customers to get the first outcome working, the handoff from them to customer success and value teams becomes one of the most important moments in the lifecycle. That handoff is where outcomes are kept or quietly lost, and it’s the part of the lifecycle I’m most drawn to.

What I’m still thinking about

  • If proof moves before the sale, what’s left for customer success to prove after it?
  • How do you build value literacy across a team without every conversation turning into a sales pitch?
  • Which of these ten themes will still be on stage at TSW 2027?

My bet for the last one is value literacy. It’s the least glamorous of the ten, which usually means it’s the one that sticks.

These are my personal reading notes and reflections, not the views of any employer.
