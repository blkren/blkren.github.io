I find where customer value breaks, put a number on what it’s costing both sides, and build the fix. I’ve done it across a SaaS book and by building customer success from scratch in a professional services business, including winning back clients who had already decided to leave.

I think of every customer relationship as a value chain: what’s promised at the sale, what’s delivered, what the customer actually does with it, and what they’d tell their leadership it was worth. Somewhere along that chain, value usually breaks or gets stuck. Finding where, and why, is the work I enjoy most.

I’ve done it on both sides of customer success: running renewals and expansion across a SaaS book, and building customer success from scratch in a professional services business, including winning back clients who had already decided to leave.

The answers usually come from conversations with clients and the people doing the work every day. I trace the break back to where it starts, work out what it’s costing both sides, and build the fix. I get the data right before reaching for AI, and I believe candour builds trust. Before tech, I competed for GB in bobsleigh, then ran my own sports performance consultancy as I moved on from an unplanned retirement. It taught me that timing beats insight: athletes change when they’re ready, not when you’re right. It’s why I treat every onboarding and implementation as a change programme, sequenced around the people as much as the technology.

How I work

  1. 1

    Find the break

    Follow the customer from promise to reality, and listen to the people doing the work. That’s usually where the problem shows itself.

  2. 2

    Size it

    Put a number on what it costs in revenue, risk or effort, and make sure the data holds up before building on it, AI included.

  3. 3

    Fix it

    Design the process, data and conversations that close the gap. The working files are in each case study.

  4. 4

    Keep watching

    Outcomes fade quietly after implementation, so build in the signals that show it’s still working.

Then back to the start: what the signals show becomes the next break to find.
